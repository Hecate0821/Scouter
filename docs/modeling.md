# Modeling and data

How Scouter turns a configuration into kernel times, and how its data points are stored. Back to [README](../README.md).

## Where the numbers come from

The ground truth is the code, PRs and docs of SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, Megatron-LM, torchtitan, TorchRL, slime, Miles and AReaL, plus public blogs and papers. The appendix lists the modeling method. Every module page links to the files its assumptions follow.

The architecture explorer has no formulas of its own. It takes the same kernel-by-kernel step model the inference and training pages use, groups the kernels into attention and MLP modules, and divides by the number of layers each kernel covers.

- **Inference** uses an engine-neutral reference profile. It includes only optimizations that the engines all turn on by default, and runs on one GPU without CUDA-graph padding.
- **Training** uses Megatron-Core defaults on one GPU, without recompute.

The roofline model:

- **Time per kernel:** max(FLOPs / (peak × 0.7), bytes / (HBM bandwidth × 0.8), kernel floor).
- **Ridge point:** peak × 0.7 / (HBM × 0.8).
- **Peaks used:** attention cores run at the BF16 peak. GEMMs run at the peak for their weight precision, with FP8 and MXFP4 / NVFP4 mapped to the FP8 and FP4 peaks.
- **Per kernel class:** in inference, GEMMs and attention cores take their efficiency from a small table by class, precision and GPU generation (Hopper, Blackwell SM100, Blackwell Ultra SM103). Each cell names the public or measured number it comes from, for example cuBLAS BF16 on B200, FlashAttention-3 / 4, FlashMLA, DeepGEMM and CUTLASS SM103 NVFP4. Setting `kEff` to 0 goes back to the flat 0.7 / 0.8.
- **Kernel-level overheads:** 0.45 µs of idle time between kernels inside a CUDA graph, removed by programmatic dependent launch (`pdl`), following [vLLM #64846](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/64846) and [FlashInfer #8135](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer/pull/8135); NVFP4 / MXFP4 MoE pads the expert hidden size to a multiple of 256 on SM100 / SM103, following [vLLM #39510](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/39510).
- **Serving benchmark:** `serve` runs `conc` requests with `isl` input and `osl` output tokens in a closed loop. TPOT is the decode step plus the other requests' prefills spread over the output. Against the 16 calibratable SGLang cookbook rows (GLM-5.3-Flash on GB300, Kimi K3 on B300; rows that are a single burst of requests are left out), the median predicted / measured TPOT is 1.00 and 10 rows are within ±25%. MTP at concurrency 1 is about 2.3× too fast, because per-step scheduling and draft overheads are not modeled. Measured TTFT is 95–185 ms above the model, which covers only GPU time.

The mechanism diagrams reimplement the visual ideas of Attention Lab, credited under each diagram and in the [README](../README.md#acknowledgements).

## What a parallel layout changes

Every key either changes the result or is reported as inert: `Train.inert(m, c, g)` and `Infer.inert(m, c, g)` return `{ key: reason }` for this config, and the racks gray those controls out and show the reason. `tests/config_reach.js` holds both directions: an inert key changes nothing, and every other key changes a result somewhere.

- **Linear attention under CP** follows `linCP`, for GDN and KDA alike. `chunkwise` / `state` is `flash-linear-attention`'s `cp_context` (what Miles uses): each rank all-gathers `[S_ext | M]`, shape `[Hv, dk, dv + dk]` in FP32, forward and backward, twice as many slots when a rank holds more than one sequence, plus the short-conv halo. It also pays for `M` per value head: `2·dk²` per token for `KᵀW`, `2·dk³ / 64` per token for the chain product at TF32x3 (about a third of the peak), and `(cp − 1)·2·dk²·dv` to merge, twice each in backward. `headwise` / `ulysses` does all-to-all of the in_proj and norm outputs and needs CP to divide the value heads.
- **Pipeline stages:** layers split evenly and `lm_head` + CE sit on the last stage, so the stage time is `head + (rest) / pp`. The ideal basis allows moving layers, `max(total / pp, head)`. `pp·vpp` above the layer count is invalid.
- **Load imbalance** (`imb`) exists only across an EP group: it does nothing at `ep = 1`, at one inference card, or when experts are TP-split.
- **CP overlap.** Ring and all-gather expose the first chunk and hide the rest behind (C−1)/C of the attention core. Ulysses all-to-alls can also overlap when chunked by head: the ideal basis counts that overlap so it stays a lower bound, and the modeled basis conservatively counts them as fully exposed. SMs taken by communication work (packing, copying) are not modeled separately.
- **CP buffers** follow the method: all of a document's K / V for all-gather, two chunks for ring, the head-wise Q / K / V for Ulysses, and the halo for sliding-window layers. K / V heads split under TP, a latent does not.
- **FSDP contention:** TP and linear-CP traffic share the link lanes with FSDP. When the lane is the slower side, compute changes stop moving `tMB`, so curves flatten there.
- **Two-batch overlap** (`tbo`, SGLang `--enable-two-batch-overlap`, vLLM `--enable-dbo`) applies only to MoE backends with a separate all-to-all (`standard`, `deepep`, `hybridep`) and at least two requests (or two prefill tokens). The modeled basis splits the padded batch into two micro-batches and runs the whole layer sequence for each, so each reads the weights and launches its kernels and all-to-alls on its own (`tboMerge` in `infer.js`). One micro-batch's all-to-all hides behind at most `etaTbo` (default 0.5) of the other's compute. In prefill the second half's prefix includes the first half. Weight reuse in L2 between the two halves is not counted, so the cost at low concurrency is an upper bound. The ideal basis does not split: the whole batch's all-to-all hides behind the whole batch's compute, so it stays a lower bound. `Infer.tboSweep` gives the on / off comparison on the deployment page.
- **Bounded replay** (`replay`, CSA models only, prefill only). SGLang turns it on by default (`--enable-decoder-swa-bounded-replay`), and vLLM has the same split. The model runs in two pieces, added in sequence (`seqMerge` in `infer.js`; DP sync counts once). The early layers run the whole chunk. The late layers run only the last `W` tokens of each request that finishes in this chunk (`reqPerChunk`), eagerly. SGLang cuts at the last kv source and still writes that layer's compressed KV for the whole chunk; vLLM cuts one layer later. SGLang turns replay off under DP attention + DeepEP. Replay is not numerically identical to a full prefill, so for RL rollouts whose logprobs must match the trainer, set `replay: false` (`--no-enable-decoder-swa-bounded-replay`).
- **Engram** follows SGLang's `--engram-config` (docs `advanced_features/engram.mdx`). `engramAt` is the placement: `gpu` row-shards the tables over the instance, `host` keeps them in host memory and reads them at `hw.host`. `engramRepl` is the replication: `none` (one shared copy) or `per_node` (a full copy per node, which needs the instance to fit in one node; otherwise it is flagged invalid). `engramLookup` is how the rows come back: `auto` picks `peer_read` for GPU tables inside one NVLink domain, otherwise `all_reduce` under TP and `all_to_all` under DP attention when the 24 columns divide over the GPUs; `all_to_all` moves 1/tp of the bytes. `engramStage` copies the rows of a shared host table on a side stream during the previous forward (`engram_staging.py`, `SGLANG_OPT_DSV41_ENGRAM_HOST_STAGING`); it applies only to prefill and extend batches and only without ATS (not on Grace), and only the part longer than the whole step shows (`exposedStage`). `engramDt` is the table format (`mxfp8`, or `mxfp4` via `quantization_config.engram_embed_quant`). These four options are SGLang's; vLLM keeps the older model (`cpu_offload` reads host rows at `hw.host`). In training, the Engram tables are left out of dense parameters, gradients, the optimizer and gradient sync: the tech report updates them as sparse rows in host memory, and this is an assumption here, not something checked against a training run.
- **mHC** (`m.mhc`, DeepSeek-V4.1) costs one op twice per layer: a projection of the `n·D` residual (`n` hyper-connection streams) to `(2 + n)·n` mixing coefficients in FP32, then the `n`-stream mix and update.
- **HSI** (`hsi`, training, CSA models with a candidate set). The tech report introduces hierarchical sparse indexing in post-training (§2.3.2): with `hsi` on, the reindex layers score at most `blocks × bs` candidates (2048 × 8 = 16,384 for V4.1-Flash); with it off (pretraining), they score the whole causal context.
- **DSpark** (`specMethod: "dspark"` on a model that has a `drafter`) uses the model's own drafter config: its main projection, its layers (attention + MoE) and its lm head, with its weights counted in memory. Its verification is confidence-scheduled (tech report §2.4.3): `specVer` is the average share of the drafted tokens sent to verify, so the verify width is `1 + round(k · specVer)`, and an `accept` above that width is flagged invalid. SGLang's DeepSeek-V4.1 rejects TBO, PP and speculation other than DSpark, and these are flagged as invalid.
- **Prefill** runs `lm_head` and sampling only on the chunk that finishes a request. Above 256 chunks, each sampled chunk stands for its neighbors, and `ttft` still covers the whole prompt.

- **Speed of light** (decode): `r.solT` is the step's total bytes over the HBM peak, divided by `1 + accept` with speculation; the inference page shows how far the step is from it.
- **DCP** (`dcp`, vLLM `-dcp`, SGLang `--dcp-size`) deals the full-attention KV round-robin by token over `dcp` GPUs of the attention TP group, so the per-rank KV of MLA / DSA / CSA divides by `dcp` and GQA's replicated heads divide likewise (`dcp ≤ tp / Hkv`, vLLM only). Each decode layer all-gathers q in the DCP group, then merges the partial outputs: `ag_rs` all-gathers the fp32 LSE and reduce-scatters the output by head (bf16 in vLLM, fp32 in SGLang); `a2a` packs output and LSE into one bf16 all-to-all (`dcpComm`, empty = the engine's default: SGLang `a2a`, vLLM `ag_rs`). Prefill all-gathers the prefix KV in the group. Linear-attention state is per request and does not shrink. DCP has no effect (and is marked inert) without full attention, without attention TP, or for GQA outside vLLM; it is invalid when it does not divide the attention TP degree, for GQA above `tp / Hkv`, and for CSA under vLLM. `Infer.dcpSweep` gives the sweep on the deployment page.
- **Colocated prefill** (`Infer.coloc`, the recommended-config comparison on the deployment page): each decode step finishes `tokPerStep / osl` requests per DP rank and takes in as many `isl`-token prompts. Separate scheduling adds their whole prefill time to the step and stalls decode for a whole prompt (one chunk with `pdInterval`); mixed scheduling (vLLM V1, SGLang `--enable-mixed-chunk`) adds each prefill token's marginal time inside a full-budget mixed step (`Infer.mixStep`, `C − B` prefill rows, minus its pure-decode part) and stalls decode for one such step. SGLang's mixed chunk is off for the DeepSeek-V4 family and with speculative decoding.
- **Chunked prefill sweep** (`Infer.chunked`, CLI `deploy`) compares two schedules for each chunk size `C` with the current decode batch `B`, prompt `P` and output `osl`. Separate (SGLang's default): a prefill pass stalls decode for the whole prompt, or for one chunk with `pdInterval` (SGLang `--prefill-decode-interval`), which adds `(chunks − 1) · pdInterval` decode steps to TTFT. Mixed (vLLM V1, TensorRT-LLM, TitanRL, SGLang `mixChunk` = `--enable-mixed-chunk`): each step carries all decodes plus `C − B` prefill rows, built by `Infer.mixStep` from the same operators (linear layers once over all rows, attention, the KV write and `lm_head` per part), so TTFT is `⌊P / (C − B)⌋` full mixed steps plus one step carrying only the leftover rows. The steady state must absorb `B · P / osl` prefill tokens per decode step; the mean TPOT spreads that over the steps. Queueing, prefix hits and simultaneous prompts are not counted. A mixed step can cost more than its parts when the combined rows lose a row-count fusion (SGLang fuses AR + RMSNorm only up to 2048 rows).
- **Layouts on G GPUs** (`Infer.layouts`, CLI `deploy`): G / t independent TP t instances; hybrids with attention TP a × DP N / a and MoE EP over all N GPUs (SGLang `--tp N --attn-dp-size N/a`, where an all-to-all backend forces `ep_size = tp_size`; vLLM TP × DP with `--enable-expert-parallel`, EP = TP × DP); DEP (a = 1); and TP or hybrid with DCP. In the hybrids each attention-TP rank dispatches only its 1 / a of the tokens and the combined tokens are all-gathered back in the TP group; SGLang returns through all-reduce + dp_scatter when attention TP > 1, which is not modeled separately. TensorRT-LLM's attention DP cannot be TP-split inside a rank, so its shape is DEP only. `Infer.layoutPoint` gives one layout at a per-GPU concurrency `b` (the attention rank runs `b · a`), split into attention, MoE and communication, with the MoE rows per expert and the GEMM intensity against the ridge.
- **Prefill / decode disaggregation** on the deployment page adds the KV transfer to TTFT (`Infer.pdXfer`): the per-request KV at the input length, over NVLink when both instances fit in one NVLink domain, otherwise over the NIC.
- **KV cache page**. SGLang does not cache CSA's sliding-window KV: on a prefix hit it prepends the last `min(prefix, W)` tokens to the extend and recomputes them (`encoder_swa_replay.py`), and only that window is regenerated. `copyUs` is a fixed cost per copy call (one per page per KV layer), for loaders that copy page by page; vLLM batches its copies (`swap_blocks_batch` with `cuMemcpyBatchAsync`), so the default is 0. zartbot/gfd measured per-call costs of about 1.5–5 µs. `snapEvery` keeps a linear-attention state snapshot every that many tokens (SGLang `--mamba-radix-cache-strategy extra_buffer`, `--mamba-track-interval` 256); each snapshot is stored in the cache, and 0 keeps only the one at the end of the sequence. For CSA models the page also shows the per-token KV going from V4-Flash's layout to V4.1's FP8, MXFP8 and NVFP4 layouts (`KV.csaSteps`).
- **Router flips** (RL page): with a per-layer flip probability `p` between rollout and training, a token sees `nMoe · p` flipped layers on average and at least one with `1 − (1 − p)^nMoe` (`Common.routeFlips`), assuming layers are independent. That is an upper bound: the R3 paper measures about 10% of routers flipping and 94% of tokens with at least one flip on Qwen3-30B-A3B's 48 layers.
- **Wave quantization is not modeled.** For persistent grouped GEMMs and large prefill tiles its effect on compute time is under about 2%; decode GEMMs are memory-bound and use split-K / stream-K; and the per-class efficiency table already absorbs what is left.
- **RTX PRO 6000 Server** (SM120, GDDR7, no NVLink) runs in its own 8-GPU PCIe topology (`pcie8`, `Common.homeTopo`). NVIDIA's product page lists FP4 4 PF, FP8 2 PF and BF16 1 PF without saying whether those include sparsity. The RTX Blackwell architecture whitepaper puts the sparse number after the slash (RTX 5090 FP4 1676 / 3352); scaled to 188 SMs and about 2.6 GHz, dense FP4 is about 2 PF, so the table halves the product-page numbers. SM120 has block-scaled FP4 / FP8 MMA (so `sm >= 100` format checks pass) but no tcgen05 / TMEM: kernels written only for SM100 / SM103 (V4.1's FP4 KV layout, trtllm-gen MoE padding, Dist-MoE) are off there (`Common.sm10x`). It has no per-class efficiency cells yet, so it uses the flat 0.7 / 0.8.

Not verified against a measurement: the 2.5× backward factor of the attention core, and the factor of 2 for `M` in backward, which counts both sides of each matrix product.

## Across NVLink domains (the Network page)

`Common.link(hw, topo, n, stride)` decides what a group of `n` ranks, `stride` apart, gets. Inside one NVLink domain all three kinds of traffic get `hw.nvl`. When the group spans domains:

- **Rings** (all-gather, reduce-scatter, all-reduce): NCCL sends each channel out of the domain through a different NIC (rails), so a group with `d` members per domain gets `min(d · NIC, NVLink)`. Before the cluster pages, a cross-domain group was charged one NIC for everything, which made FSDP across HGX nodes about 8× too slow. No preset crossed a domain, so no other page changed.
- **Point-to-point** (pipeline stages, ring attention, short-conv halos): the boundary rank's own NIC.
- **All-to-all**: the traffic to this domain, to other domains in the same segment, and to other segments goes over NVLink, the NIC, and the NIC plus the spine; the slowest part sets the time.
- **The spine** (`topo.net`, only set by the Network page): a segment is leaf downlinks × NICs per node. Leaves have `ports / over` uplinks. With adaptive routing the uplink load is the average; with ECMP the busiest uplink holds `E[max]` of `F` flows hashed onto `U` ports (a fixed-seed Monte Carlo, `Common.ecmpMax`). In NCCL's ring order a ring leaves a segment once per channel, so rings barely load the spine. All-to-all and pipeline traffic do.
- **In-network reduction**: NVLS inside a domain and SHARP across domains make an all-reduce send one copy and receive one (the same formula the ideal basis already uses). All-gather and reduce-scatter are not credited.

`Fabric.apply` turns the cluster settings into `hw` / `topo`: NVLink times the available fraction (PCIe x16 when there is no NVSwitch), and the NIC capped by PCIe x16 with GPUDirect RDMA or by the host link without it. On a PCIe-only machine an inference TP all-reduce over more than 2 GPUs is an NCCL ring: vLLM and SGLang turn off their custom all-reduce there (`custom_all_reduce.py` in both). Training scales weakly: `N` and the global batch grow by the same factor. The defaults reproduce the main pages exactly (`tests/fabric.js`). The time split is counterfactual: the time saved by an infinitely fast, zero-latency NIC is credited to the NIC, then the same for NVLink.

**Model vs public measurements.** The Network page feeds public nccl-tests, point-to-point and all-to-all measurements (Crusoe, CoreWeave, NCCL issue #1801, TensorRT-LLM blog 18, fabric-lib) to the same `link` / `arT` / `a2aT` with the same GPU count, message size and topology (`Fabric.bench`). The efficiency parameters were not refit to them. What they show: large IB messages run at about 0.71–0.77 of the measured bandwidth in the model (η<sub>N</sub> = 0.7 is low there), small messages across nodes are about 5× too slow (α across nodes is too large), NVLS and SHARP are about 1.4–1.5× too optimistic, ring bandwidth does not degrade at 1440 GPUs as it does in the measurement, and all-to-all over NVLink is within 7%.

Not modeled: per-hop switch latency (no public numbers), the PCIe penalty across CPU sockets, congestion control and lossy fabrics, NCCL's tree and PAT algorithms, and how PXN routes traffic between rails.

## Data points

```
{ schema: 1,
  sets:   { <id>: { title, origin: "anon" | "public", stage, hw, gpus, topo, model, conditions, dims: [...], metrics: { <name>: { unit, label } } } },
  points: [ { id, set, ...dims, ...metrics } ] }
```

- **Anonymized points** (`origin: "anon"`) are measured numbers. They keep only the measurement conditions and the numbers, with no source. All points in a set share the same dims and metrics. The `id` is built from the dims.
- **Public points** (`origin: "public"`) come from public PRs, blogs, papers, release notes and docs, and carry `src` / `url`. The build computes the weight `w` of each quantization evidence point from recency × hardware match × source type. The reference date is `sets.quant_evidence.as_of`.
- **Confidence:** every set is `measured` (our anonymized sets), `reported` (a number stated upstream) or `derived` (computed from published numbers, or measured under a simulated condition). A row can override its set's. Public sets carry `as_of`, the date they were curated or the upstream commit was read.
- **Releases** are LMSYS blog posts and SGLang / vLLM / slime / Miles / AReaL GitHub releases from 2025-11 to 2026-10. Each is keyed `releases:<project>:<version>`. Pages look up release links by project and version, and a missing entry is an error.

The build fails if:

- an anonymized point carries `src` / `url`;
- an anonymized set's description contains source-like words;
- an id is duplicated;
- a set is empty.

To add a public number, add a row (`id`, `date`, `src`, `url`, numbers) to the matching file in `data/public/`. To add a measurement, add a JSON file to `data/anon/` with a title, conditions, dims, metrics and points. Then rebuild:

```
uv run tools/build_db.py                     # regenerate data/db.json and src/data/db.js
uv run tools/build_db.py --check             # only check that the generated files are current
uv run tools/build_db.py --sqlite x.sqlite   # also export SQLite (one view per set) for ad-hoc queries
uv run --no-project tools/import_cookbook.py --ref <sha>        # re-import the SGLang cookbook benchmark rows
uv run --no-project tools/import_ncu.py report.csv --name b200_moe --hw B200 --dtype fp4 --conditions "..."
```

`tools/import_ncu.py` documents the `ncu --csv --page raw` command to run first. Each kernel becomes a point with its duration and DRAM bytes; with `--flops` it also gets analytic FLOPs. `Scouter.kernels --set <name>` then gives each kernel's SOL efficiency, and the profile (tool version, clocks, replay mode, metrics) is kept with the set.

Pages read points with queries like `DB.where("cp_full_model", {model: "qwen35", cp: 8})`. Data text is written in the data files; translations of it go in `locales/<lang>/data.js` (see [docs/i18n.md](i18n.md)).

## Online profiling (the Profile page)

`Profile.split` takes an `Infer.step` result and regroups it the way a live trace does, as seconds per step on an average rank:

- **kern's three classes** (pegainfer-project/kern, `kern_device_seconds_total`): compute (operator kernels), comm (NCCL kernels and kernels that use a peer buffer, so waiting for other ranks counts here), idle (gaps between kernels, graph launch, CPU launch, DP sync, an exposed Engram copy).
- **The categories of the DuckDB queries in susun's CUPTI HES post**: MoE, attention, attention projections, indexer, mHC, other, waiting in MoE, between steps; linear attention, dense MLP and speculative decoding get their own rows.
- **EPLB**: `Infer` sizes MoE for the busiest GPU (`imb`); the step is run again with `imb = 1`, and the difference is the time lost to expert imbalance.
- **DPLB**: `dpSkew` is the slowest DP rank's attention-side work over the mean. Ranks meet in the MoE communication, so each waits `(dpSkew − 1)` × the attention-side time on average, and the step grows by that much. The post measures this as the same MoE call's duration minus the shortest across ranks.

`Profile.observer` bounds the cost of always-on tracing: kernels per step (graph nodes with CUDA graphs) × the per-node cost measured in the post on GB300 (0.6 µs with HES, 0.8 µs with legacy CUPTI). It is an upper bound: with long kernels and the GPU ahead of the CPU, part of it hides. The post measured +115 µs per decode step at concurrency 1 and +64 µs at concurrency 48 for Qwen3-4B. HES itself (CUPTI §2.3.6) is Blackwell-only, process-wide, cannot be turned off once on, and drops records when its fixed 128 MB buffer fills.
